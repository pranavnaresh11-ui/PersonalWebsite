Robotics, optics, & adaptive systems.

I work on robots that move without wheels or legs: vibration-driven tensegrity structures that learn, through Bayesian optimization and careful measurement, how to cross ground they have never seen. I also build optical instruments, most recently a Background-Oriented Schlieren sensor that makes heat and gas flow visible to a small on-device model, along with the software that holds these projects together. When I'm away from the bench, I'm usually on a golf course, thinking about the mechanics of a swing.

About

I'm a student in Cary, North Carolina, doing independent robotics research alongside dual-enrollment college coursework.

Most of my time goes to hardware: designing, wiring and testing the machines I study, then writing the code that measures them. My current focus is gait adaptation in tensegrity robots, and making the Mirage sensor small and inexpensive enough to be useful in manufacturing.

Research & work

Tensegrity Locomotion2026 A vibration-driven tensegrity robot that tunes its own gait with Bayesian optimization. Computer vision tracking and IMU telemetry measure every trial, so the robot can adapt as the ground beneath it changes.

The goal is cross-terrain gait adaptation: one robot, no wheels or legs, finding a way to move efficiently over surfaces it was never tuned for. A longer write-up and the code will be linked here.

Mirage Optical Sensor2026 A modular Background-Oriented Schlieren imaging system paired with an edge-AI model. It makes thermal plumes and shielding-gas defects visible, so problems in manufacturing can be caught as they happen.

Schlieren imaging reveals tiny changes in how air bends light. Mirage puts that technique into inexpensive, modular hardware and runs detection on the device itself. Build notes and results will be linked here.

Applied AI & Full-Stack Systems2025 Web and mobile tools built end to end in TypeScript, React, Python and C++. Most of them began as something I needed for my own research and grew from there.

Selected projects will be listed here. In the meantime, the code is on GitHub.

Autonomous Robotics2024 Mechanical design, wiring and autonomous code for FRC and VEX competition robots. It is where I learned that a machine is only as good as its least reliable connection.
